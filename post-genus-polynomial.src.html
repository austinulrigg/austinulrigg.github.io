On the Genus Polynomial of Cubic Graphs

View on arXiv → Download PDF

The Heffter-Edmonds-Ringel theorem states that every cellular embedding of an orientable graph is determined up to homeomorphism by a rotation system and vice versa. A rotation system prescribes at each vertex a cyclical permutation of its incident edges. For a cubic graph, each vertex has exactly two possible orders, and hence a cubic graph on $n$ vertices has $2^n$ rotation systems.

From Duke's interpolation theorem, it is known that every connected graph has an embedding on each orientable surface from its minimum to maximum genus. Since each rotation system corresponds to an orientable embedding on some surface, it is a topic of interest to consider the distribution of such embeddings.

We record this distribution by the genus polynomial,

$$\Gamma_G(x)=\sum_{g\ge 0} N_g(G)\,x^g,$$

where $N_g(G)$ is the number of rotation systems of $G$ whose embedding has genus $g$. The least exponent of $\Gamma_G$ is the minimum genus of $G$.

For example, the triangular prism graph has genus polynomial of $\Gamma = 2 + 38x + 24x^2$. This means that of its $64$ rotation systems, two embed on the sphere, thirty-eight on the torus, and twenty-four on the double torus.

Matroids and spectra

In our study of this distribution for cubic graphs, our first result is that, for $2$-connected cubic graphs, the genus polynomial is a cycle matroid invariant: If $M(G) \cong M(H)$, then $\Gamma_G = \Gamma_H$. For non-cubic graphs, the genus polynomial is in general not a cycle-matroid invariant.

Our second result is that the genus polynomial and the adjacency spectrum are incomparable. We exhibit connected cubic graphs on $16$ vertices sharing adjacency spectrum, number of spanning trees, girth, diameter, vertex and edge connectivity, order of the automorphism group, and every cycle length count through length $10$, yet their genus polynomials are pairwise distinct.

When considering the expected face count of a uniformly random rotation system of a given graph, we show further that faces shorter than twice the girth are determined by the adjacency spectrum but longer faces are not.

We also exhibit an infinite family of cospectral cubic pairs $(G_t, H_t)$ on $14+2t$ vertices with pairwise distinct genus polynomials.

Finally, we computed the genus polynomials of all $7{,}875{,}918$ connected cubic graphs through $22$ vertices. The full paper is here.
